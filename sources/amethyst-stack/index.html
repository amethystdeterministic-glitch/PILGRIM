Beyond Probabilistic Systems

Modern computational systems increasingly underpin decisions where error, opacity, and non-reproducibility carry material consequences. Yet they remain probabilistic and heuristic by design, evaluated through statistical confidence rather than guarantees enforced at execution.

The Problem: Structural Weaknesses

Non-Repeatable Behaviour

Identical inputs may yield divergent outputs without clear causal explanation.

Compromised Auditability

Internal state transitions are opaque, emergent, or reconstructed only after execution.

Reactive Governance

Harm must occur before it can be addressed. Failure is managed, not prevented.

The Solution: Deterministic Runtime Enforcement

Fail-Closed Execution

When confronted with ambiguity, execution halts. No unlawful output is produced.

Structural Interdiction

Unlawful execution paths are removed from the execution space before materialization.

Governance at Runtime

Obligations are not merely declared; they are enforced at the point of execution.

Core Components

Invariant Definition Kernel

Defines what states may exist and which transitions are even theoretically possible. Immutable at runtime.

Deterministic Enforcement Engine

Binary evaluation: admissible or inadmissible. No fallback logic or probabilistic tolerance.

Pre-Execution Constraint Filter

Silently removes unlawful execution paths from the execution space entirely.
